## Gaps and limitations found in this domain

The dominant pattern in open finance datasets is **tabular-numeric with no natural-language state**: German Credit, Taiwanese Bankruptcy, Default-of-Credit-Card-Clients, ULB Credit Card Fraud (deliberately PCA-anonymized), and Give Me Some Credit are all rows of numbers with a label — useful for the `choice`/`score`/`noul` *label space*, but a real mismatch with a language-conditioned decision model's `state` input. Building `state` text for these requires synthetic templating ("Applicant is 34, employed 3 years, checking balance <0 DM, requesting a 24-month loan of 5,000 DM...") which introduces fabricated framing that should be clearly labeled as synthetic-narrative-over-real-labels, not naturally-occurring text. The datasets that avoid this problem (CFPB complaints, StockNet's tweets, Lending Club's desc/title fields, FiQA's headlines) are comparatively rare and should be weighted heavily.

Second gap: **large (10k+) datasets with clean, unambiguous licenses are scarcer than they look.** Several well-known "classic" finance Kaggle sets carry either an outright unclear license (Give Me Some Credit's blank/"Unknown" field) or are Kaggle *competition* data (Home Credit Default Risk, IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection, Porto Seguro's Safe Driver Prediction all fall under Kaggle's competition rules restricting use to "the Competition, academic research and education, and other non-commercial purposes") — excluded these three rather than list with an inflated license claim. Similarly, the Company Bankruptcy Prediction Kaggle mirror of the Taiwanese data explicitly states "Data files © Original Authors" (restrictive) — used the original CC BY 4.0 UCI release instead.

Third: **no verified, real, open dataset for algorithmic-trading-signal classification or financial-statement-fraud classification** turned up with a clear license and accessible schema — proprietary vendors (Numerai, most alt-data trading-signal sets) or academic papers with no public data release dominate that sub-area; dropped rather than padded. ESG scoring similarly has almost nothing beyond small (~500-row) company-level snapshots — real methodology-vendor ESG scores (MSCI, Sustainalytics) are licensed and not freely redistributable.
