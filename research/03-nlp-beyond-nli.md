## What this domain tells us about building a schema-general decision model

The single clearest signal across these 26 datasets is that **"classification" in production text systems almost never means a fixed 3-way schema — it means an unpredictable, per-deployment option set that ranges from 2 to 150+ classes, is sometimes multi-label, and is sometimes not discrete at all.** BANKING77 and CLINC150 alone span 77 and 150 intents respectively (CLINC150 also encodes an explicit "none of the above" class, exactly the OOS/refusal behavior a `noul`-style primitive needs to learn generically). GoEmotions pushes further into 28-way *multi-label* territory with a real-world skewed distribution, which a fixed-schema NLI-tuned model has no mechanism to represent at all. At the other extreme, Civil Comments and App Reviews show many real decisions aren't "pick one of N" — they're continuous calibrated scores or ordinal ratings, meaning a genuinely general decision layer needs to move fluidly between `choice`, ordinal `score`, and multi-label `choice`, not just widen a softmax. The CFPB and customer-support-ticket datasets are the most directly diagnostic for the "zero items matched" JevBench/jabr-v2 failure: exactly the support-routing/triage shape production systems actually face, with hierarchical, dataset-specific label vocabularies (52 queues, 18 CFPB product lines) no amount of NLI fine-tuning would expose a model to. Licensing adds a practical lesson too: even within one well-known collection (TweetEval) the license isn't uniform — must be checked per subtask — and several datasets carry non-commercial (CC-BY-NC, Yelp Agreement) or research-only (dair-ai/emotion) restrictions that would quietly disqualify commercial redistribution if not flagged. Practical implication for better-jev-bench/ekVachan: train and eval on label sets described *at inference time* (names + optional descriptions), not baked into the output head, and include both very-large (CLINC150, GoEmotions, 20-Newsgroups) and very-small (SNIPS, SMS Spam) schemas in the same corpus so the model generalizes the *mechanism* of picking among N arbitrary options, not the specific N it saw in training.
